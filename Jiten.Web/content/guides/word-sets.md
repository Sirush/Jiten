Characters names, place names and particles that you know for sure but never bothered adding to a deck can be present hundreds of times in decks, dragging your coverage down from what it should be. Word sets are an easy way to deal with those in a simple click.

## Available word sets

Go to **Settings** and open the **Word Sets**. Here's the different sets available:

| Set | Size        | What is in it                                                                                |
|---|-------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Particles & Common Grammar** | ~400 words  | Particles and the grammar words that hold sentences together, recommended unless you're a total beginner                                 |
| **Kaishi 1.5k** | ~1500 words | The vocabulary from the Kaishi 1.5k starter deck, recommended unless you're a total beginner |
| **Names** | 300k+ words | Every kind of name at once, and broader than the three sets below put together               |
| **People** | 50k+ words  | Names of people, like Shinzo Abe or Suzuki Airi                                              |
| **Places names** | 200k+ words | Place names, like Minato Ward or Tokyo                                                       |
| **Other names** | 10k+ words  | Companies, organisations, groups, products                                                   |
| **Onomatopoeia** | ~1300 words | All onomatopoeia                                                                             |
| **Counters** | ~250 words  | All counters                                                                                 |

Each card also shows a form count, which is higher than the word count because most words are written more than one way. Press **Vocabulary** on any set to read through exactly what is inside before you choose.

## Blacklist or Mark as Mastered

Pick one of the two from the dropdown on the set's card, then press **Subscribe**.

Both count every word in the set towards your coverage, and neither will ever turn a word into a card for you to study. So the choice is purely personal, depending on how you want to consider the words:

- **Mark as Mastered** for words you genuinely know. These also fill in your kanji grid and show on your public profile.
- **Blacklist** for everything you are happy to never see again. Names are the obvious case as they can affect your coverage a lot. These stay out of your kanji grid and off your profile.

If two sets you subscribe to contain the same word, it counts as **Mastered**.

::note
A set only covers words you have no card for. If you already have a card for a word, even if you've only reviewed it once, the set does not count it towards your coverage.
::

## Changing your mind

You can **Unsubscribe** from a set at any moment. It doesn't affect your existing cards in any way.

To swap a set from **Blacklist** to **Mark as Mastered** or back, unsubscribe first, then subscribe again with the other option.

::tip
Recommended if you already have some experience with Japanese: **Mark as Mastered** for **Particles & Common Grammar** oand **Kaishi 1.5k**, **Blacklist** the **Names** ones unless you plan to study them, then [import your known words](/guides/tracking-known-words) from whatever you were using before.
::
