A word can stick better when the explanation is yours. You can attach a note or mnemonic to any word and your own example sentences to any reading, which will be displayed during your SRS studies.

Both need you to be logged in.

## Your own note

Open a word page and look under **Meanings** for **Add your own notes**. Fill in what you don't get from the dictionaries: a nuance, a mnemonic, anything hthat will help you remember it.

- Up to **500 characters**.
 You can format it with `**bold**`, `*italic*` and lines starting with `- ` for a list. A **Preview** appears under the box as you type.
- The note belongs to the word, so every form of it shows the same note.

Saving again overwrites the note, and you can **Delete** it at any time.

On your study cards the note appears as the **Custom notes** block, which sits on the back of the default layout. You can rearrange it or remove it under **Settings → Study (SRS)** with **Customise layout (advanced)**.

## Your own example sentences

A sentence from something you actually watched can beat a random sentence from the corpus thanks to the memories you have associated to it.

You can add up to **3 custom sentences per reading**, or **10 with [Jiten+](/jiten-plus)**. Each one can be up to 150 characters, with an optional source line.
There's 3 ways to add one:

- **Star a sentence you like.** The example sentence become yours and you can edit it as you want. It will be shown on the vocabulary page and during your SRS studies.
- **Edit it first.** The pencil opens an editor with the sentence loaded, so you can edit it before saving. The original is left alone.
- **Write your own.** The pencil beside the **Example sentences** heading opens **Custom Example Sentences**, where you can add, edit and delete them freely.

Wrap the word you want highlighted in double asterisks, like `**食べる**`.

The star and the pencil are both there during reviews too, so a sentence you like on a card can be kept without leaving the session.

::note
Once a reading has one custom sentence, its cards show one of yours at random and stop showing corpus sentences by default. You can still read the corpus ones behind **See more sentences** on the card.
::

Mining a word together with its sentence from [Jiten Reader](/guides/browser-extension) add the example sentence as well as the source directly.

::tip
Pick a sentence where the target word is the only thing you do not understand (i+1).
::
